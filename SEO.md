# TRANS STAR: SEO plan

Goal: be the first result a NZ business finds when it needs chilled, frozen or dry goods moved around
Auckland, and turn that visit into a call or quote request.

Written October 2026, when transstar.co.nz first went live (new domain, no backlinks, no reviews yet).

---

## 1. What "#1" realistically means

Nobody can guarantee a #1 position. Google ranks on relevance, local proximity, reputation (reviews, links)
and how long a site has proved itself. What we can control is every one of those inputs. The plan is built
around where a 2-truck business can actually win:

| Battle | Who holds it now | Can TRANS STAR win it? | When (estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Maps / local pack for "refrigerated transport near me", "refrigerated transport auckland" | Big carriers with Maps listings | **Yes.** Local pack weighs proximity, category match and reviews more than company size | 1 to 3 months after the Business Profile is verified and reviews start |
| Long-tail phrases: "frozen freight nz", "chilled freight auckland", "small transport companies auckland", cost questions | Mostly nobody targets them well | **Yes**, with dedicated pages | 1 to 3 months after indexing |
| Head term in normal results: "refrigerated transport auckland" | Hall's, Refrigafreighters, GK Logistics, Big Chill: older domains with many links | Page 1 is realistic; #1 needs reviews and links over time | 6 to 12+ months |
| Informational: "what temperature should frozen food be transported at" | AU/UK government and food-safety sites | Possible for the NZ-flavoured version; brings links and awareness, few direct quotes | 3 to 6 months |

## 2. How the research was done

No paid keyword tool (Ahrefs, Semrush) was available, so three free signals were combined:

1. **Google autocomplete, NZ locale** (`hl=en-NZ, gl=nz`), 80 seed phrases. Autocomplete only lists phrases
   people search often, and orders them by popularity, so presence and position are a demand signal.
2. **Live search results** for the main phrases: who ranks and how strong they are (competition).
3. **Intent:** would the person searching book a truck? Consumer, job-seeker and equipment-hire searches are excluded.

**Limitation:** this gives *relative* demand, not monthly numbers. To get numbers, open Google Keyword Planner
(free with a Google Ads account, no spend needed), paste the keywords in section 4, location New Zealand.
Paste the results back and the priorities can be rebalanced. After launch, Google Search Console shows the
real searches the site appears for; that data replaces estimates within 4 to 8 weeks.

### What the data says

- New Zealanders search **"refrigerated transport"**, not "chilled transport". Google even rewrites
  "chilled transport auckland" to "refrigerated transport auckland". This is the head term.
- "Chilled" and "frozen" are searched with **"freight"** and **"NZ"**: "chilled freight nz", "frozen freight nz",
  "chilled freight auckland", plus "chilled courier nz", "frozen courier nz".
- A gap that suits a small operator: **"small transport companies auckland"**, "small freight companies auckland",
  "small trucking companies auckland". Big carriers can't credibly target these.
- **Cost questions** exist with almost no good NZ answers: "refrigerated transport cost", "refrigerated courier cost",
  "how much does pallet delivery cost".
- **"Pallet delivery auckland / nz / near me / cost"** is the strongest dry-goods phrase; "dry goods delivery"
  on its own is mostly searched outside NZ.
- **"Near me"** variants are common ("refrigerated transport near me", "refrigerated courier near me"):
  these are won through Google Maps, not the website alone.

## 3. Site structure

One page could only rank for one or two phrases, so the site grows to five pages, each owning a cluster.

| URL | Owns (primary keyword) | Supporting phrases | Title tag (≤ 60 chars) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/` | refrigerated transport auckland | refrigerated transport nz, small transport company auckland, chilled and frozen transport, transport companies auckland, cost questions | Refrigerated Transport Auckland \| Chilled & Frozen \| TRANS STAR |
| `/frozen-freight-auckland/` | frozen freight auckland | frozen freight nz, frozen transport nz, frozen food transport, frozen courier nz | Frozen Freight Auckland \| Frozen Food Transport \| TRANS STAR |
| `/chilled-freight-auckland/` | chilled freight auckland | chilled freight nz, chilled transport nz, chilled courier nz | Chilled Freight Auckland \| Chilled Transport \| TRANS STAR |
| `/dry-goods-delivery-auckland/` | dry goods delivery auckland | pallet delivery auckland, pallet delivery nz, pallet delivery cost, ambient freight | Dry Goods & Pallet Delivery Auckland \| TRANS STAR |
| `/chilled-frozen-transport-temperatures/` | what temperature should frozen food be transported at | chilled food transport temperature, cold chain nz, how to transport frozen goods | Chilled & Frozen Transport Temperatures (NZ Guide) |

Rules that keep pages from competing with each other:
- Each phrase has exactly **one** owner page. Other pages link to it with that phrase as the link text.
- No suburb-by-suburb pages ("refrigerated transport North Shore" etc.). With one depot those are thin
  "doorway" pages that Google penalises. Suburbs are covered by one honest "Areas we cover" list.

## 5. On-page rules

For every page:
1. **Title:** primary keyword first, benefit second, brand last. Under 60 characters where possible.
2. **Meta description:** 140 to 155 characters, keyword once, a concrete fact, and the call to action with the
   phone number. This is the "ad" in the results, so it is written for clicks.
3. **One H1** containing the primary keyword, in the site's own voice.
4. **First 100 words** use the primary keyword and the place ("Auckland") once, naturally.
5. **H2s** carry the supporting phrases where they read naturally; never stuffed. Read aloud test: if it
   sounds like a robot, rewrite it.
6. **Link text** between pages is descriptive ("frozen freight in Auckland"), never "click here".
7. **URLs** short, hyphenated, keyword-based, trailing slash.
8. **Images** (when photos arrive): filename and alt text describe the photo honestly and include the
   service where true, e.g. alt "TRANS STAR refrigerated truck at a loading dock in Auckland".
9. **Facts only.** Content rules in CLAUDE.md still apply: no invented numbers, clients or claims.

## 6. Structured data (JSON-LD)

| Page | Types |
|---|---|
| All | `LocalBusiness` (one shared `@id`), with locality Ellerslie, region Auckland, phone, email, hours, area served, service catalogue |
| `/` | `WebSite` (gives Google the site name "TRANS STAR" above the result) |
| Service pages | `Service` (provider = the business `@id`, areaServed Auckland), `BreadcrumbList`, `FAQPage` |
| Guide | `Article`, `BreadcrumbList`, `FAQPage` |

Note: Google stopped showing FAQ rich results in 2023 to 2026. FAQ markup is kept because it is valid
schema.org that search and AI systems still read, but it won't add drop-downs in Google.

The street address stays off the site and markup, matching the Business Profile set as a service-area
business with a hidden address. Only "Ellerslie, Auckland" is shown.

## 7. Speed, mobile and engagement

Already in place: static HTML, CSS and JS inlined (about 17 KB gzipped), one preloaded font, AVIF/WebP photos
with responsive sizes, no layout shift, mobile-first layout, sticky call bar on phones.

What we do for click-through, time on site and bounce:
- **Titles and descriptions written as ads** (section 3), with the free first delivery and phone number.
- **Answer first.** Each service page opens with what the visitor came for (temperatures, days, loads).
- **FAQs that match real searches**, including cost, so visitors don't leave to find answers.
- **Clear next step on every screen:** Call or Request a quote, same two buttons everywhere.
- **Internal links** between related pages (frozen ↔ chilled ↔ dry goods ↔ guide) so a visitor's second
  question is one click away.
- **No pop-ups, no interstitials, no autoplay.**

Honest note: "bounce rate" and "time on site" are not direct Google ranking factors. They matter because a
visitor who finds the answer and calls is the goal; Google rewards pages that satisfy searchers over time.

Targets (check with PageSpeed Insights after each release): Performance ≥ 95 on mobile, SEO 100,
Accessibility ≥ 95, LCP < 2.0 s, CLS < 0.05.

## 9. Citations and links (reputation)

Consistent name, phone and website everywhere: **TRANS STAR LTD · 022 075 1526 · https://transstar.co.nz ·
Ellerslie, Auckland**.

1. **NZBN register** (business.govt.nz): add the website and phone to the company's NZBN record. Free, official.
2. **Bing Places** (import from Google Business Profile) and **Apple Business Connect**.
3. **Facebook page** and **LinkedIn company page**, both linking to the site.
4. NZ directories: Yellow NZ, Finda, NZ Business Directory, Hotfrog NZ, Cylex NZ.
5. **Customers and suppliers:** ask existing customers who list their carriers or partners to link to the site.
6. **Industry:** Transporting New Zealand (membership includes a member listing), local business association
   (Ellerslie / Penrose). Only where membership makes sense for the business anyway.
7. Never buy links or join link schemes.

## 10. Measurement

1. **Google Search Console:** verify `transstar.co.nz` as a Domain property (TXT record added in Netlify DNS),
   submit `https://transstar.co.nz/sitemap.xml`, request indexing for all five pages.
2. **Bing Webmaster Tools:** import from Search Console.
3. **Monthly check** (15 minutes): Search Console → Performance: clicks, impressions, average position for the
   keywords in section 4; Business Profile → Performance: calls, website clicks, searches.
4. **KPIs:** calls and quote requests per month (the only number that pays the bills), then impressions and
   position for priority A keywords.
